# Item attribute battery

You read one menu item or packaged food product and answer one closed question
per attribute below: does this item have the attribute? Answer from the item's
own words only: its section, its name and its description.

For each attribute answer exactly one of:

- yes: the item's words say it has the attribute, or name a food that always
  has it. Put in "words" the exact words from the item that say so, copied
  character for character, as few as make the case.
- no: only when the item's words settle that it does not, in one of two ways:
  the item is a single plain food whose nature rules the attribute out (an
  avocado is not fried; plain water has no sugar), or its words say so outright
  ("unsweetened", "no salt added", "plain", "nut-free"). Put in "words" the
  words that settle it.
- unknown: everything else. A menu description names the main parts of a dish,
  not every ingredient, so a description that does not mention an attribute
  does not rule it out. A dish or product made of several things is unknown
  for an attribute its words do not name. Anything usually served alongside
  but not named (a dipping sauce, wasabi, dressing, a garnish) makes the answer
  unknown, not no. The absence of a word is never evidence of absence.

A named food counts only for what it always is. Tempura is always fried; a
croissant is always a pastry; a jalapeno is always hot. A dish that often but
not always has something (pad thai and peanuts, a burger and a sesame bun) is
unknown for it unless the words say so. Use no knowledge of a particular
restaurant, store or brand beyond the words given.
